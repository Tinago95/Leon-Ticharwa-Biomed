## What this project is

Take one small bacterial enzyme, dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) from *Escherichia coli*,
and one real antibiotic that blocks it, trimethoprim, and work the full beginner
drug-discovery pipeline end to end: sequence, AI-predicted structure, comparison with a
crystal structure, ligand preparation, molecular docking with AutoDock Vina, interaction
analysis, and publication-quality visuals.

It is deliberately a **training target**. The answers are already known and published,
which is exactly the point: at every stage you can check your work against reality. When
the pipeline reproduces a known result, you have learned something real. When it does
not, you have found a bug worth understanding. Also real learning, and material for the
write-up.

### What this project is not

It is not drug discovery. No new compound is found, no patient-relevant claim is made, and
a docking score is not a measurement of how well a drug works. Saying so clearly, in your
own write-up, is part of the project.

## The science, in one place

### The job DHFR does

Cells need a constant supply of tetrahydrofolate (THF), the active form of folate,
because THF carries the one-carbon units used to build thymidylate and purines, the
letters of new DNA. Every time thymidylate is made, a THF is spent and left behind as
dihydrofolate (DHF). DHFR is the recycling enzyme: it converts DHF back to THF using the
reducing cofactor NADPH.

```
7,8-dihydrofolate + NADPH + H+  ->  tetrahydrofolate + NADP+
```

Block DHFR and the THF pool drains. DNA synthesis stalls, and a dividing bacterium stops
growing. That is the whole therapeutic idea, and it is also why DHFR inhibitors can harm
fast-dividing human cells, bone marrow and gut lining, when selectivity fails or folate
stores are low.

### Anatomy you need to recognise

- **Overall fold:** a central eight-stranded beta sheet flanked by four alpha helices. The active-site cleft splits the protein into two subdomains.
- **Adenosine-binding subdomain (residues 38–88):** holds the adenosine end of the NADPH cofactor.
- **Met20 loop (residues 9–24):** a flexible loop that closes over the active site during catalysis. Its position, closed, occluded or open, is the classic example of protein motion mattering to function, and the region where a predicted structure deserves the most scepticism.
- **F-G loop (116–132) and G-H loop (142–150):** the other two loops framing the pocket.
- **Asp27:** the key acidic residue in the *E. coli* active site. It anchors the protonated diaminopyrimidine ring of trimethoprim. If your docked pose does not interact with Asp27, treat the pose as wrong until proven otherwise.

> **Verified.** The fold description is confirmed from `1RX2`'s own header annotation: one
> sheet (`A`) declaring 8 strands, and 4 HELIX records. Residue 27 is Asp and residue 20 is
> Met in both the UniProt sequence and the `1RX2` coordinates.

## Trimethoprim, selectivity, and the clinical thread

### The drug

Trimethoprim is a diaminopyrimidine: a pyrimidine ring carrying two amino groups, linked
by a methylene bridge to a trimethoxyphenyl ring. It is a **competitive inhibitor**: it
sits in the folate-binding site and physically prevents DHF from binding. Its
diaminopyrimidine ring mimics the pterin ring of the natural substrate, which is why the
Asp27 interaction matters: in the bacterial enzyme at physiological pH, trimethoprim is
substantially protonated on that ring, and the charged form is what the active site
grips.

### Why it mostly spares human cells

- Trimethoprim binds bacterial DHFR far more tightly than the human enzyme. Small differences in the active site, accumulated over the sequence that differs, do the work.
- Bacteria must make their own folate; human cells take folate from the diet. The partner drug sulfamethoxazole blocks an earlier, bacteria-only step in folate synthesis, so the pair block the pathway at two sequential points, the classic "sequential blockade" behind co-trimoxazole.
- Selectivity is not absolute. In people who are already folate-depleted, trimethoprim can contribute to megaloblastic changes in the marrow. That caveat belongs in your write-up.

### The contrast drug: methotrexate

Methotrexate inhibits the same enzyme but is aimed at the *human* protein, and is used
against cancers and, at lower doses, autoimmune disease. You will dock it as a control:
it should score strongly against human DHFR. Two drugs, one enzyme family, opposite
therapeutic intent. That comparison is the most interesting paragraph of your write-up,
and it costs you one extra docking run.

### Resistance, briefly

Bacteria escape trimethoprim mainly by acquiring plasmid-borne genes for drug-resistant
DHFR variants, by overproducing the target, or by reducing drug entry.

> **Note.** `P00382`, which the original plan misidentifies as human DHFR, is itself one
> of these resistance enzymes (*Dihydrofolate reductase type 1*, gene `dhfrI`). It is
> not stored in this repo, to remove any chance of reaching for the wrong
> accession. Fetch it on demand if you take on the resistance stretch task.

### Reading pLDDT

| Band | Reading |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Very high, backbone and side chains usually trustworthy |
| 70–90 | Confident, backbone good; check side chains in the pocket |
| 50–70 | Low, treat as a suggestion; do not dock into it blindly |
| Below 50 | Very low, often disordered; leave it out of conclusions |

**Rule for this project:** the DHFR active site, including Asp27 and the Met20 loop, must
sit in the 70+ bands before you dock.

AlphaFold stores pLDDT in the B-factor column of the model file, which is why it can be
extracted with BioPython:

```python
from Bio.PDB import PDBParser
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

model = PDBParser(QUIET=True).get_structure("af", "data/raw/AF-P0ABQ4-F1-model_v6.pdb")
residues = list(model[0]["A"].get_residues())
plddt = [sum(a.bfactor for a in r) / len(list(r)) for r in residues]

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 2.6))
plt.plot(range(1, len(plddt) + 1), plddt, lw=1.4, color="#0f4c5c")
plt.axhline(70, ls="--", color="#d97a2b")
plt.axvspan(9, 24, color="#d97a2b", alpha=0.15)   # Met20 loop
plt.xlabel("Residue number"); plt.ylabel("Mean pLDDT")
plt.savefig("figures/plddt_profile.png", dpi=300, bbox_inches="tight")
```

Then open the PAE plot: low PAE (dark) between two regions means their relative position is
confidently predicted; high PAE across the Met20 loop marks its placement as uncertain even
if its local shape scores well. Log one sentence about each.

   > `P0ABQ4`**. That is real, not a parsing bug.

3. **Colour the model by pLDDT** and screenshot the superposed pair. The regions where the
   two structures disagree should be the regions pLDDT warned you about, most likely the

4. **Repeat against the human model and `1U72`** as a visual exercise: the point is to *see*
   that the fold is the same while the pocket surface differs.

### How to interpret the number

| Backbone RMSD | Reading |
|---|---|
| Under 1 Å | Essentially the same backbone. Remaining differences are side chains and loops. |
| 1–2 Å | Same fold, meaningful local differences. Normal for a good prediction vs a holo crystal. |
| Over 2 Å | Stop and diagnose: wrong chain, mismatched residue numbering, or a genuinely flexible region dominating the average. |

## Phase 3: Trimethoprim, prepared properly

Start from the SMILES string, never from coordinates pulled out of a crystal structure.
Building from SMILES is what makes the later redocking test honest.

```python
# Verified against PubChem CID 5578: canonicalises identically under RDKit.
TMP_SMILES = "COc1cc(cc(c1OC)OC)Cc2cnc(nc2N)N"

from rdkit import Chem
from rdkit.Chem import AllChem
mol = Chem.AddHs(Chem.MolFromSmiles(TMP_SMILES))
AllChem.EmbedMolecule(mol, randomSeed=42)
AllChem.MMFFOptimizeMolecule(mol)
Chem.MolToMolFile(mol, "data/prepared/tmp_3d.mol")
```

> **Verified.** Trimethoprim is PubChem **CID 5578**, formula **C14H18N4O3**: **21 heavy
> atoms**, 39 atoms with hydrogens. The original's SMILES string is **correct**: RDKit
> canonicalises it and PubChem's to the identical form.

- **Protonation is the decision that matters most.** Trimethoprim's diaminopyrimidine ring is substantially protonated at physiological pH, and the protonated form is what Asp27 binds. Prepare the protonated form for the main runs; run the neutral form once as a comparison and log how the pose and score change. Record the choice in `notes/decisions.md`.
- **Convert to PDBQT with Meeko:** `mk_prepare_ligand.py -i data/prepared/tmp_3d.mol -o data/prepared/tmp.pdbqt`

  > **`[CORRECTED]`** The original says to expect "roughly five to six" rotatable bonds.
  > The real path, RDKit embed then Meeko 0.8.0, gives **7 rotatable branches**. Three
  > methoxy torsions, the methylene bridge and the ring-bridge bond account for it.
  > **Expect 7**; the original's range will look like a failure when it is not.

- Prepare methotrexate the same way as the human-target control, and dihydrofolate as the natural-substrate reference.

## Phase 4a: Receptor preparation: one recipe, used everywhere

1. Take the crystal or model PDB; keep one chain; remove waters and the bound ligand, **except** keep NADPH/NADP+ in the receptor for the main runs, because trimethoprim binds alongside the cofactor, not instead of it. State this choice in `decisions.md`; it is the most common DHFR docking error.
2. Add hydrogens at pH 7.4, check Asp27 is deprotonated (charged), it must be, to bind protonated trimethoprim.
3. Convert with the ADFR/Meeko receptor tools or OpenBabel to `receptor.pdbqt`. Use the identical recipe for crystal, AlphaFold and human receptors, or the comparison means nothing.

> **Note on cofactor state.** The *E. coli* entries supply NADP+ (`NAP`); the human `1U72`
> and *S. aureus* `2W9G` supply NADPH (`NDP`). If the recipe is meant to be identical
> across systems, note the difference rather than assuming it away.

## Phase 4b: The docking runs, in the only defensible order

### Run 1: Redocking validation (do this first, always)

Take **`2W9G`** (*S. aureus* DHFR crystallised with trimethoprim **and** NADPH). Remove
trimethoprim, prepare the receptor with the Phase 4a recipe, centre the search box on
where the crystal ligand sat, and dock the SMILES-built trimethoprim back in. Then
measure the heavy-atom RMSD between your top-ranked pose and the crystal pose, with
RDKit or a PyMOL RMSD calculation against the extracted crystal ligand, *not* with the
RMSD columns in Vina's own log, which only compare poses with each other.

> **`[CORRECTED]`** The original's command passes `--log`. **That flag was removed in Vina
> 1.2**: it does not appear in `vina --help`. The command as written fails. Redirect
> stdout instead.

```bash
# Box: centre on the crystal ligand centroid, 22 A per side for DHFR.
# Compute the centroid in 02_compare.py from the crystal ligand atoms - do not guess it.
vina --receptor data/prepared/2w9g_receptor.pdbqt \
     --ligand data/prepared/tmp.pdbqt \
     --center_x <from script> --center_y <from script> --center_z <from script> \
     --size_x 22 --size_y 22 --size_z 22 \
     --exhaustiveness 32 --num_modes 9 --seed 42 \
     --out results/redock_2w9g.pdbqt \
     > results/redock_2w9g.log
```

### Pass check 4a: the gate for the whole project

Top-ranked redocked pose within **2.0 Å** heavy-atom RMSD of the crystal pose,
reproduced with at least two different random seeds. If it fails, fix preparation,
protonation or the box. Do not proceed to novel docking on a protocol that cannot
reproduce a known answer.

### Runs 2–5: only after the gate passes

| Run | System | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Trimethoprim into *E. coli* crystal DHFR (`1RX2`-based, cofactor kept) | Cross-docking: does the validated protocol transfer to a structure solved with a different ligand? |
| 3 | Trimethoprim into the *E. coli* AlphaFold model | The headline comparison: same ligand, predicted vs experimental receptor. Compare pose, score and Asp27 interaction, and discuss the Met20 loop honestly. |
| 4 | Trimethoprim into human DHFR (`1U72`-based / human model) | Selectivity: expect a weaker score and/or a different pose. Methotrexate into human DHFR is the positive control alongside it. |
| 5 | Sensitivity repeats | Each main run repeated with seeds 1, 2, 3 and exhaustiveness 16 vs 32. A result that appears under one seed only is not a result. |

**Reading Vina scores:** the score is a heuristic estimate in kcal/mol, useful for ranking
poses of the same ligand in the same receptor. Differences under about 1 kcal/mol are noise.
Never compare a score in one protein directly against a score in another as if it were a
measured affinity, in the selectivity run, report the numbers *and* that caveat in the same
table.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Redocking RMSD well over 2 Å | Wrong protonation of trimethoprim, or box not centred on the crystal site | Rebuild the ligand protonated; recompute the box centre from the crystal ligand centroid; retest |
| Good RMSD but pose flipped end-to-end | Scoring ambiguity in a near-symmetric pocket | Check the Asp27 interaction; a flipped pose that misses Asp27 is wrong regardless of score |
| Trimethoprim docks outside the pocket | Box too large, or centred on the whole protein | Shrink to about 22 Å per side centred on the site; never blind-dock for the main runs |
| Scores look great in every system, including human | Cofactor removed, so the ligand is using the cofactor's space | Rebuild receptors with NADPH/NADP+ retained, as in Phase 4a |
| Vina error on the receptor file | Non-standard atoms or missing hydrogens from preparation | Re-prepare cleanly; inspect the PDBQT; check Asp27 and histidine states |
| AlphaFold-model docking much worse than crystal | Met20 loop or a pocket side chain placed differently in the prediction | Expected, and reportable: identify the residue, show it in a figure, discuss, do not hide it |
| Results change every run | Single seed, low exhaustiveness | Seeds 1–3, exhaustiveness 32, report the spread, that spread is part of the result |
| RDKit will not embed the ligand | Bad SMILES or a sanitisation failure | Re-copy the SMILES from PubChem; print the molecule and atom count before embedding |
| pLDDT extraction gives nonsense values | Averaging over the wrong atoms, or reading a crystal file (real B-factors) | Extract only from the AlphaFold model file; average per residue as in the Phase 1 script |
| PyMOL alignment RMSD looks perfect but the picture is wrong | Alignment fitted to a subset or wrong chain | Align chain A to chain A on CA atoms; visually inspect the superposition before trusting the number |
| **`unrecognised option '--log'`** | **Vina 1.2 removed `--log`** | **Redirect stdout: `vina ... > results/run.log`** |
| **Downloaded AlphaFold file is 134 bytes** | **EBI CDN returned 403 to curl's default User-Agent** | **Send a browser User-Agent; check file sizes after fetching** |

**Logging rule:** every failure above that you hit gets a learning-log entry with the
symptom, the cause you eventually found, and the fix. Those entries are the most-read part
of a beginner write-up, because they prove the work was real.

Never drop the redocking gate or the write-up.

### Write-up structure

1. **Why I did this.** Two or three sentences, in your own words.
2. **What I built.** The pipeline in one paragraph plus the repo link.
3. **How I know it works.** The redocking result, with its RMSD number and overlay figure. This section is your credibility, lead the results with it.
4. **What I found.** AlphaFold vs crystal comparison; bacterial vs human docking table, with the score caveat in the same breath.
5. **What went wrong.** At least two real failures from your log and what each taught you.
6. **What this does not show.** Docking is not efficacy; a predicted structure is not an experiment; you are a beginner and this is a training exercise.
7. **What I am learning next.** The AChE + donepezil project, and the gaps you intend to close.

**Claims discipline:** write "I reproduced", "the protocol passed", "the score was
consistent with", never "I discovered", "the drug binds with X affinity" from a docking
score, or "AI designed".

## Bridge to Project 2: acetylcholinesterase + donepezil

Everything transfers: the repo layout, the environment, the preparation recipe,
redocking-first discipline, interaction analysis and figure style. What gets harder is
the point of doing it second: the target is human, the binding site is a deep narrow
gorge rather than an open cleft, donepezil is larger and more flexible than
trimethoprim, and the clinical story is Alzheimer's disease. A setting where the limits
of docking as a model of a brain drug need even more careful wording. Start it only when
this project's write-up is published.

### Glossary

**Ångström (Å): a tenth of a nanometre, the unit of atomic distances. **RMSD:** average
distance between matched atoms after superposition; smaller means more similar. **PDBQT:**
the file format Vina reads, carrying atom types and charges. **SMILES:** a text string
encoding a molecule's structure. **Redocking:** docking a ligand back into its own crystal
structure to test whether a protocol can reproduce a known pose. **pLDDT / PAE:**
AlphaFold's per-residue confidence and its estimate of error in relative positions between
residues.
